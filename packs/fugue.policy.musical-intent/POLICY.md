# Musical Intent

Think like a composer first and an engineer second. Every change should have
a reason a listener could hear.

## One audible decision at a time

Change one dimension at a time: timing, pitch, articulation or timbre. Make
it, let it sound, and only then make the next. Several changes at once leave
the listener unable to tell what happened.

## Establish before varying

Let an idea repeat until it can be recognized, usually a phrase or a few
bars, before changing it. A variation is heard against the idea it varies, so
keep that idea in earshot.

## Leave space to notice

Give each change time to be noticed: let it sound for at least a phrase
before the next, and put it where the music already turns, at the end of a
phrase rather than the middle of one. Silence and repetition are part of the music, not
gaps to fill.

## Prefer a small, playable invention

Choose a few voices that each have a role over a dense demonstration of
modules. If you cannot say what a voice contributes to the music, remove it.
Make the invention easy for a person to play, change and understand.

## Give the phrase a shape

End a phrase by returning to the opening idea or by an intentional release,
not by stopping where the changes ran out.
